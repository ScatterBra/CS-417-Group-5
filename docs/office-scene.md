# Office Escape Room — `Assets/Scenes/office.unity`

Contributor: Rundong He. Branch: `RundongHe_office`.

The whole scene is generated by [OfficeEscapeRoomBuilder.cs](../Assets/Editor/OfficeEscapeRoomBuilder.cs).
Run **Tools → CS417 Group 5 → Build Office Escape Room** to rebuild it from scratch; the
menu item clears `office.unity` and re-creates every object, so the layout is reviewable
as code instead of as hand-placed objects.

## Premise

It is 22:41 and the badge reader is down. The office door will not open until the three
things left lying on the middle desk are put back where they belong.

## The three Key Props and their Locks

| Key Prop | Lock | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Coffee mug (`Key_Mug`) | Drip tray on the vending machine (`Lock_Coffee`) | West wall |
| File folder (`Key_File`) | Open drawer of the filing cabinet (`Lock_Files`) | East wall |
| Cordless handset (`Key_Handset`) | Its own charging cradle (`Lock_Phone`) | North wall, on the desk |

All three start loose on the centre desk with Rigidbodies, so they drop, settle and can be
knocked around before the player picks them up.

## Signifiers

### Grab signifiers (X2.1)

Every object the player can pick up — and **nothing else in the room** — breathes with the
same warm amber glow: a pulsing emissive material plus a small amber point light parented to
the prop, driven by [GrabSignifier.cs](../Assets/Scripts/Office/GrabSignifier.cs).

Two things make this readable. First, all three Keys are palm-sized everyday desk objects
(a mug, a folder, a phone handset) — things a person already expects to be able to pick up,
while the furniture around them is not. Second, the whiteboard by the door states the rule
outright: **"AMBER GLOW = you can pick it up."** The metaphor carries most of it; the
whiteboard removes any doubt on the first attempt.

The glow also stops the moment a Key reaches its Lock, so the pulse only ever means "this one
is still yours to move" — the player can tell at a glance what is left to do.

### Escape signifiers (X2.2)

1. The room has exactly **one opening**, framed in bright yellow trim — the everyday "this is
   the way out" of a fire door or an emergency exit.
2. A solid door panel blocks it, and the status panel beside the door reads **"DOOR LOCKED"**
   in red with **"0 / 3 PUT BACK"** underneath.
3. That panel carries three red lights captioned **MUG**, **FILES**, **PHONE** — one per Lock.
   The captions name the objects the player is holding, so the panel tells them both *how many*
   Locks matter and *which* objects solve them, without naming a location.
4. Each Lock station also has its own red lamp, visible from across the room. Red-blocks-you /
   green-lets-you-through is the same convention as a traffic light or a turnstile.

So a player who looks at the door learns: there are three things to put back, and they are the
mug, the files and the phone.

### Lock signifiers (X2.3)

Each Lock holds a **translucent blue ghost of exactly the object that belongs there**, sitting
in the pose it will end up in — the mug standing on the drip tray, the folder standing upright
in the open drawer, the handset seated in its cradle. The whiteboard teaches the second half of
the rule: **"BLUE OUTLINE = something belongs here."**

This works because the ghost is not a symbol that has to be decoded — it is the object itself,
at the right size, in the right place. Matching a held object to its own silhouette is the same
thing people do with a jigsaw piece, a toolbox shadow board or a plug and its socket.

On top of the ghost, each station repeats a real-world pairing the player already knows:

- a mug goes under a drinks machine,
- a folder goes in a filing cabinet drawer,
- a handset goes back in its base.

A Lock only ever accepts its own Key. `LockReceptacle` is registered as an `IXRSelectFilter` on
both the socket and the Key, so dropping the mug at the filing cabinet does nothing at all — the
socket will not close around it. That keeps the signifier honest: if it looks like it fits, it
fits, and if it does not look like it fits, it will not go in.

### Repetition and variety in Lock mechanics (X2.4)

**The common thread: every Lock is an object being put back where it belongs.** Nothing is a
combination, a code or a switch — the whole room is one verb, repeated three times. What changes
is *how much work the player does to identify the partner*:

1. **Mug → drip tray.** Pure shape. The ghost sits on a tray the exact width of the mug, and the
   tray is empty and obviously waiting. This is the teaching lock: it establishes "carry it over,
   let go, it seats itself."
2. **Folder → filing cabinet drawer.** Shape plus category. The drawer is pulled open on a
   cabinet full of closed drawers, so the player has to read the furniture — "this is where paper
   lives" — not just match an outline.
3. **Handset → cradle.** Shape plus a pair. The cradle is a separate small object on a desk full
   of other small objects (monitor, keyboard, lamp). The player has to notice that the handset and
   the base are two halves of one device. This only lands because locks 1 and 2 have already
   installed "everything here has a home" as the way to think about the room.

The difficulty rises, but the action never changes — which is the point.

### Eased state changes (U1)

Every state change runs through
[EasedStateChange.cs](../Assets/Scripts/Office/EasedStateChange.cs), which interpolates along an
`AnimationCurve.EaseInOut` — flat at both ends, fast through the middle — rather than snapping.

When a Lock is solved, four things ease at once:

- the blue ghost fades to zero alpha while growing slightly, then switches itself off;
- the station's lamp eases from red to green;
- the bezel on the station eases from red to green emission;
- the matching light on the exit panel eases red → green **and** swells past its target scale and
  settles back, so the change reads from across the room.

When the third Lock is solved, the door panel eases 1.62 m sideways over 1.8 s after a 0.35 s
beat, the doorway strip eases to green, and the readout changes to "DOOR OPEN — GO HOME".
Walking through the doorway eases the "YOU ESCAPED" banner in with the same pop.

## Where each rubric item lives

| Rubric | Where |
|---|---|
| W1 Key Props with grab affordances | `Key_Props/Key_Mug`, `Key_File`, `Key_Handset` — each has `XRGrabInteractable`. Both hands carry an `XRDirectInteractor` (added into each controller's `XRInteractionGroup`) alongside the stock near-far interactor. |
| W2 Solid objects | All three Keys have non-kinematic Rigidbodies; the floor, walls, desks and props all have colliders. The Keys visibly drop and settle on the desk at scene start. |
| W3 Locks with accepting affordances | `Lock_Coffee`, `Lock_Files`, `Lock_Phone` — `XRSocketInteractor` + `LockReceptacle`, each accepting one Key. |
| X1 Escaping the room | `Escape Controller` counts all three `LockReceptacle`s, opens the door, and `Escape_Landing/Exit_Trigger` ends the run when the player walks out. |
| X2.1 / X2.2 / X2.3 / X2.4 | See above. |
| U1 Eased state changes | `EasedStateChange` on every ghost, lamp, bezel, exit-panel light, the door panel and the banner. |

## Scripts

| File | Job |
|---|---|
| [LockReceptacle.cs](../Assets/Scripts/Office/LockReceptacle.cs) | A Lock: accepts exactly one Key, solves once, and stops the Key being taken back out. |
| [EscapeController.cs](../Assets/Scripts/Office/EscapeController.cs) | Counts solved Locks, opens the door, drives the readouts. |
| [EasedStateChange.cs](../Assets/Scripts/Office/EasedStateChange.cs) | Reusable eased transition: position, rotation, scale (with pop), colour, emission, light, fade. |

| [EscapeExitTrigger.cs](../Assets/Scripts/Office/EscapeExitTrigger.cs) | Fires the win beat when the player walks out through the open door. |

## Walkthrough video checklist

The rubric requires the video to show all three Key Props sitting in their Lock colliders before
the escape triggers. A run that covers everything:

1. Start facing the door — read "DOOR LOCKED", "0 / 3 PUT BACK" and the three red lights.
2. Turn to the desk, show the three amber-glowing Keys, knock one so it tumbles (W2).
3. Carry the mug to the vending machine, show the blue ghost, release — ghost fades, lamp eases
   to green, exit panel light 1 pops green, readout reads 1 / 3.
4. Repeat for the folder and the handset.
5. Pan across all three Locks with their Keys seated, then to the panel showing 3 / 3.
6. Show the door easing open, walk through, land on "YOU ESCAPED".
